Proper water chemistry protects swimmers and equipment. In Spring, TX, high evaporation and hard source water make balance especially important.

## The Key Numbers

| Parameter | Ideal Range | Why It Matters |
|-----------|-------------|----------------|
| Free Chlorine | 1–3 ppm | Kills bacteria and algae |
| pH | 7.2–7.6 | Comfort + chemical effectiveness |
| Total Alkalinity | 80–120 ppm | Buffers pH swings |
| Calcium Hardness | 200–400 ppm | Prevents scale or plaster etching |
| Cyanuric Acid | 30–50 ppm | Protects chlorine from sunlight |

## Common Local Challenges

- **High CYA** from repeated dichlor or trichlor use can lock chlorine and allow algae even when the test shows “chlorine present.”
- **Rising calcium** from evaporation concentrates minerals and can scale heaters and surfaces.
- **pH drift** is common after heavy rain or when aeration is high.

## Testing Frequency

Weekly professional testing is ideal. Between visits, a simple test strip or kit helps you catch major swings. Never add large doses of multiple chemicals at once—adjust one parameter, wait, then retest.
