A green pool is frustrating—especially when the Texas sun is shining and you want to swim. The good news: most algae blooms can be cleared with the right process.

## Why Algae Appears

Green algae thrives when free chlorine drops below the level needed to kill it. Common triggers in Spring include:

- Heavy rain diluting chemicals
- High cyanuric acid (stabilizer) reducing chlorine effectiveness
- Dirty filters reducing circulation
- Missed service visits during peak heat

## The Recovery Process

1. **Test the water** — Know exact chlorine, pH, alkalinity, and CYA levels before adding anything.
2. **Brush thoroughly** — Walls, floor, steps, and tile line. Brushing breaks up the algae so chemicals can reach it.
3. **Shock the pool** — Use an appropriate dose based on volume and severity. Liquid chlorine or cal-hypo are common choices.
4. **Run the filter continuously** — Keep water moving and clean or backwash the filter as pressure rises.
5. **Vacuum dead algae** — Once it settles, remove it so it does not return.
6. **Establish residual** — Maintain free chlorine in the proper range and address any underlying issues (filter, CYA, etc.).

Severe cases may need two or three visits. We always give a realistic timeline and price before starting.
